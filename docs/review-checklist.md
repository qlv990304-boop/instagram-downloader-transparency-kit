# Instagram Downloader Review Checklist

Use this checklist when documenting or evaluating a browser-based Instagram downloader. Record only what a page states or what you test directly; mark everything else `unknown`.

## Six required disclosures

| Field | What to record | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Platform | Exact public content types accepted, such as Reels, feed videos, or IGTV | “Everything” or “all platforms” without boundaries |
| Format | Exact delivered file type and whether available audio is included | “Original file” without naming a format |
| Quality | A measurable ceiling and whether the source controls actual resolution | “HD quality” without a number |
| Login | Whether Instagram credentials, cookies, or account access are required | Treating “anonymous” as proof that no credentials are processed |
| Batch | One link at a time, multiple links, or multi-item post behavior | Leaving carousel behavior ambiguous |
| Limit | Numeric allowance, time window, file-size/time limits, and whether failures count | “Unlimited” without clarifying technical or fair-use limits |

## Access boundaries

Check whether the service clearly addresses:

- private accounts and Close Friends content;
- deleted or login-gated posts;
- Stories and Highlights;
- photo-only posts and image-only carousels;
- profile, Explore, and malformed URLs;
- region restrictions and platform rate limits.

## Responsible-use disclosures

A public post is not automatically licensed for reuse. A responsible page should tell visitors to download only media they own, have permission to save, or are otherwise legally allowed to use. It should not claim to bypass authentication or private-account controls.

## Verification notes

- Save the exact source URL and observation date.
- Separate page claims from independently tested behavior.
- Do not infer a missing feature from a search-result snippet.
- Preserve `unknown` when a page is unavailable.
- Recheck claims before using the data for current product recommendations.
